Nintendo is raising the price of the Switch 2 in the United States. Starting September 1, 2026, the console's list price climbs from $449.99 to $499.99, which means the cheapest way into Nintendo's newest system is about to cost $50 more than it does today.

---

## Why the Nintendo Switch 2 Price Increase Is Happening

Nintendo of America confirmed the revision directly, pointing to "various changes in market conditions" it expects to persist over the medium to long term. In plain terms, that's the company's way of saying rising costs are sticking around and it's passing them along rather than absorbing them.

The timing detail is the part that matters for your wallet. Right up until August 31, the standalone console holds at $449.99, and the "Choose Your Game" bundle, which pairs the console with one first-party title, sits at $499.99. On September 1, the standalone console alone moves to $499.99. So the same $499.99 that buys a console plus a game this month buys just the console next month.

This isn't a sign of a struggling launch, either. Nintendo has said Switch 2 adoption is outpacing the original Switch at the same point in its life, and it has "no particular concerns" about momentum. The hike is about cost, not weak demand, which usually means it won't be walked back.

---

## What It Means for Buyers

The cleanest way to read this is as a deadline, not a disaster. If a Switch 2 was already on your list for 2026, the next few weeks are the best window you'll get, and the bundle is the smart play. Grabbing the "Choose Your Game" bundle before August 31 effectively lands you a free first-party game compared with buying the same console for the same price in September. That's a real $50-to-$70 swing depending on the title you pick, and it's the closest thing to a discount Nintendo hardware ever offers.

If you're weighing which bundle game to start with, our [Nintendo Switch 2 bundle guide](/posts/nintendo-switch-2-bundle-pick-your-first-game) walks through the first-party options and which one gives you the most hours. Pick the game you'd have bought anyway, and the bundle math works itself out.

Some retailers have also been clearing existing stock at a discount ahead of the change, so it's worth checking current inventory rather than assuming every listing already reflects the new floor. The MSRP is the starting point going forward, not a ceiling on today's deals.

There's a bigger signal here too. Consumer electronics almost always get cheaper as they age, because components fall in price and newer models push older ones down the shelf. A console raising its list price more than a year after launch runs against that entire pattern, and it tells you Nintendo expects its costs to stay elevated rather than ease. For buyers, that flips the usual instinct on its head: the "wait a few months and it'll drop" strategy that works for phones and TVs is exactly the wrong move for the Switch 2 right now. When the floor is rising instead of falling, sooner beats later, and there's no cheaper hardware revision rumored to bail out anyone who holds off.

One group can relax: anyone who already owns a Switch 2. This is a hardware list-price change, not a games or accessories increase, and it doesn't touch what you paid.

## Buy or Wait?

If you know you want a Switch 2, buy before August 31, and buy the bundle. You'll pay today's price and walk away with a game that would otherwise cost extra in September. Waiting saves you nothing here, because unlike most electronics, this console is getting more expensive, not cheaper, and there's no cheaper revision on the horizon.

If you're genuinely unsure whether you need one, a $50 increase isn't a reason to panic-buy hardware you don't want. Sit with the decision, check our [buy-or-wait verdict for the Nintendo Switch](/buy-or-wait/nintendo-switch) for where this line sits in its cycle, and remember that a console you don't play is far more expensive than fifty dollars. The deadline only matters if you were buying anyway.
