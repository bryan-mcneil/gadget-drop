Samsung has locked in July 22 for its next Galaxy Unpacked, and two new smartwatches are expected to headline it: the Galaxy Watch 9 and the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2. If you've been about to buy a Galaxy Watch, that date is your cue to stop and hold the wallet for 48 hours.

## What's Coming at Samsung's July 22 Unpacked

Samsung officially confirmed the event. Galaxy Unpacked streams live from London on July 22 at 9 a.m. ET. The company hasn't named the watches on the invite, but the leak trail is consistent enough that the lineup is close to a given. New foldables are expected to share the stage, though the wearables are the part that matters if you're shopping in the sub-$700 smartwatch range.

Here's what the reporting points to. The Galaxy Watch 9 is said to start around $349, adding ultra-wideband for precise device tracking, a new heart health score, and hearing monitoring. Leakers put the 40mm model's battery near 400mAh, a sizable jump over the Watch 8 in the same case size. The step-up Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 is reported at $699, with an 800mAh battery (roughly 35 percent larger than the first Ultra), a brighter 3,000-nit display, and three experimental health sensors that track advanced glycation end-products, antioxidant levels, and vascular load. Both are expected to run One UI 9 Watch on Wear OS 7, and the Ultra 2 would be the debut device for Qualcomm's 3nm Snapdragon Wear Elite chip, a partnership Samsung put on record earlier this year.

Treat the prices and specs as leaks until Samsung says the words on stage. The date is the confirmed part. Pre-orders are expected to open the same day, with retail availability reported for August 5.

## What It Means for Buyers

Two things happen the moment a new Galaxy Watch launches. The obvious one: a newer watch with a bigger battery and fresh sensors arrives. The one buyers forget: the previous generations get cheaper, often fast. If the Watch 9 lands at $349, expect the outgoing models to slide on Amazon and at Samsung directly within days.

That matters because the older Galaxy Watch is already a strong buy. In [our Samsung Galaxy Watch 7 review](/posts/samsung-galaxy-watch-7-review-flagship-smarts-under-200) we called it flagship smarts for under $200, and a launch like this is exactly the kind of event that pushes that sort of deal lower still. The new health sensors on the Ultra 2 are the genuine unknown. AGEs and vascular-load tracking aren't features you'll find on any wrist today, and until independent reviewers see how accurate they are, nobody should pay $699 on the promise alone.

Who should care about the new watches? People who want the biggest battery and the newest chip, plus Ultra buyers who like the rugged titanium case. Who should ignore the hype? Anyone who mainly wants notifications, workouts, and sleep tracking. That job is already handled well by watches that are about to get a price cut.

## Buy or Wait?

Wait. Even if you have zero interest in the Watch 9 or the Ultra 2, the July 22 event is worth two days of patience. New models almost always drag down the prices of last year's watches, so holding off either lands you the newer hardware or a cheaper older one. There's no version of buying on July 21 that comes out ahead.

If you're set on the latest, watch the pre-orders that open on the 22nd. Samsung usually bundles trade-in credit and a free band or a storage upgrade in that first window, which is where the real savings on a new watch tend to hide. If you just want a great smartwatch for the least money, let the launch happen, then check the tracked price on last year's model. That's the moment the value shows up.
