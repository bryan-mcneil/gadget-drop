Samsung used its July 22 Galaxy Unpacked event in London to reveal the Galaxy Watch 9 at $379 and the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 at $699, both shipping August 7. If you've been eyeing a Galaxy smartwatch, the headline number matters: this is the third year running that Samsung has nudged the base price up, and the new watches lean on health software rather than a dramatic redesign.

## What Samsung Announced for the Galaxy Watch 9

Two watches came out of the event, not the full three-model spread some expected. The standard Galaxy Watch 9 is $379, up $30 from the Watch 8's $349 launch price last year. The rugged Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 jumps to $699, a $50 increase over the previous Ultra's $649. Both go on sale August 7, with preorders running ahead of that date.

The hardware story is incremental. Samsung fitted both watches with a new processor and larger batteries, the kind of under-the-hood update that helps endurance without changing how the watch looks on your wrist. The squircle case Samsung moved to a generation ago carries over, so the Watch 9 sits in the same design lineage as the Watch 8.

The more interesting changes are in health tracking. Samsung is adding a Heart Health Score, a single readout meant to summarize cardiovascular signals, and a new Daily Cardio Load estimate that gauges how much strain your workouts put on your body over time. Those features aim squarely at the same crowd Garmin and Apple court, buyers who treat the watch as a coaching tool rather than a notification screen. Samsung has not confirmed how much of the new health suite, if any, will reach older Galaxy watches through a software update.

## What It Means for Buyers

A $30 increase is not the story on its own. The story is what it does to the rest of the lineup. Every time Samsung launches a new flagship watch, last year's model and the one before it slide down in price, and that's where the value usually lives. The outgoing Galaxy Watch 8 and the still-capable Galaxy Watch 7 both run the same core Wear OS experience, cover the same fitness basics, and pair with the same phones. Most buyers won't feel the gap between a Heart Health Score and the heart-rate data older watches already collect.

If you want the newest sensors and Samsung's latest coaching features, the Watch 9 at $379 is the buy. If you mainly want a solid smartwatch for notifications, workouts, and sleep tracking, the arrival of the Watch 9 is your cue to grab a previous generation for less. We walked through that case in [our Samsung Galaxy Watch 7 review](/posts/samsung-galaxy-watch-7-review-flagship-smarts-under-200), and the Watch 7's value only improves as retailers clear stock to make room for the new model.

The Watch Ultra 2 is a narrower pick. At $699 it competes with the Apple Watch Ultra and higher-end Garmins, and it's aimed at people who genuinely want the bigger battery, brighter screen, and rugged case for outdoor use. For everyone else, the standard Watch 9 or a discounted older model covers the same daily needs for hundreds less.

One caution on the health features: a Heart Health Score and a Cardio Load number are estimates from wrist sensors, not medical readings. They're useful for spotting trends over weeks, not for diagnosing anything. Treat them as a nudge to move more, not as a reason alone to spend $379.

## Buy or Wait?

If you specifically want the Watch 9's new processor and health tools, wait until August 7 and buy at launch, or preorder if Samsung is bundling a trade-in or a free band. There's no discount coming in the first few weeks of a flagship's life, so waiting past launch buys you nothing here.

If you just want a good Galaxy smartwatch, don't wait, and don't pay full price for the new one. The launch of the Watch 9 is exactly when the Watch 8 and Watch 7 get cheap, and for most people those watches do 90 percent of what the Watch 9 does. Check the older model's current price, and if it's meaningfully below the $379 you'd spend on the Watch 9, that's the better deal today.
