Bose just gave its most popular headphones a serious upgrade, and the price is the headline. The QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen), announced August 6, borrow the Immersive Audio and improved noise cancellation that used to be locked to the pricier QuietComfort Ultra, but land at $359, a full $90 below the flagship. For anyone who's eyed Bose ANC and balked at the Ultra's price, this is the model that changes the math.

## The Bose QuietComfort (2nd Gen), in Numbers

Preorders opened August 8 on Bose.com and through select retailers, with shipping starting August 13. The $359 price sits squarely between the aging first-gen QuietComfort and the $449 QuietComfort Ultra.

The upgrades are real, not cosmetic. Bose reworked the noise cancellation, now branded QuietControl, around a six-microphone array and new adaptive feedforward controls. The practical win is for glasses wearers and anyone who never quite gets a clean seal, since the system compensates for gaps in the earcup fit rather than assuming a textbook one. Bose also added its TrueSpatial technology, which brings the three Immersive Audio modes, Still, Motion, and Cinema, that were previously exclusive to the Ultra line.

There's even a spec that undercuts the flagship: wired USB-C listening runs at 24-bit/48 kHz, which several outlets noted beats the Ultra's wired resolution. The design got a pass too, with a new headband, oval earcups, and more cushioning, plus limited colors like Dewdrop Mint and Rosewood Mauve alongside the usual black and white.

## What It Means for Headphone Buyers

The story here is Bose doing what Apple and Sony have done for years: taking last generation's flagship features, pushing them down a tier, and trimming the price. If you almost bought the QuietComfort Ultra for its Immersive Audio and spatial modes, you now get those same modes for $90 less. That's a genuine saving, not a marketing reshuffle.

Timing matters here, because Bose is one of the slower brands to discount a headphone in its first few months. The 2nd Gen at $359 is likely to hold that price through the fall, so treating the launch number as the real number is a safe bet rather than a sale you should wait out.

Where it gets interesting is what happens to the Ultra. Expect the older QuietComfort Ultra Headphones to see steeper discounts as retailers clear stock, which could make a marked-down Ultra the smarter buy for someone who wants the last few percent of ANC and doesn't mind last year's model. A $449 flagship that slips toward $349 on a holiday sale suddenly looks tempting next to a full-price $359 mid-tier, so watch both price tags together rather than in isolation. The 2nd Gen also squeezes the mid-range on-ear crowd. If you were weighing something like the Marshall Milton for its looks and ANC, our [Marshall Milton ANC review](/posts/marshall-milton-anc-review-is-on-ear-anc-worth-it) lays out where on-ear cans win and lose against over-ear Bose comfort, and this launch makes that call closer than it was a week ago.

One caveat to keep in mind: "flagship features for less" always means some corners were cut. Bose hasn't detailed battery life or every sensor difference versus the Ultra, so wait for the spec-by-spec breakdowns before assuming the 2nd Gen fully replaces the more expensive model.

## Buy or Wait?

Buy the 2nd Gen if you want current Bose ANC and Immersive Audio without paying Ultra money. At $359 with genuinely upgraded noise cancellation and the spatial modes people actually asked for, it's the sensible pick in Bose's lineup right now, and the wired 24-bit audio is a bonus for desk listening.

Wait if you already own the first-gen QuietComfort or a recent Sony or Bose flagship, because the jump isn't big enough to justify a fresh $359. And if the Ultra is what you truly want, hold for a few weeks: this launch is exactly the kind of event that drags the older flagship's price down, and a discounted Ultra could end up costing about the same as this new mid-tier model.
