Samsung has confirmed its next Galaxy Unpacked for July 22, and new Galaxy Watches are on the invite. If you've had a Samsung smartwatch in your cart, that date is the one that decides whether you buy this week or hold off, because a launch this size reshapes the price of everything already on the shelf.

## Samsung Galaxy Unpacked July 22: New Watches Are Coming

Samsung sent out official confirmation for a Galaxy Unpacked event streaming from London on July 22, at 9 a.m. Eastern. The company's own teaser leads with its foldable phones, the Galaxy Z Fold 8 and Z Flip 8, but it also confirms new Galaxy Watches are part of the show.

The two models widely expected are the Galaxy Watch 9, Samsung's mainstream flagship, and a Galaxy Watch Ultra 2, the rugged, bigger-battery model aimed at endurance athletes. Reporting ahead of the event points to the Ultra 2 running Qualcomm's new Snapdragon Wear Elite chip, a 3nm processor with a dedicated AI block for on-device features like smart replies and coaching. Samsung hasn't published specs or prices, so treat the model names and features as strongly expected rather than official until the keynote.

What is official is the date and the fact that the current lineup is about to have a successor. That alone changes the math for anyone shopping right now.

## What It Means for Buyers

There are really three kinds of Samsung watch shoppers this month, and the July 22 date lands differently for each.

If you want the newest hardware, the move is obvious: wait one week. Buying a current Galaxy Watch on July 21 means paying full price for a model that gets replaced the next morning. Even if you decide the new watch isn't worth the premium, seeing its price and features first costs you nothing but a few days.

If you're a value shopper, the launch is good news no matter which watch you prefer. New models push last year's watches down, and the weeks right after an Unpacked are historically when the outgoing generation sees its steepest, most reliable discounts of the year outside the holidays. The Galaxy Watch 8 and even the Galaxy Watch 7 become the smart-money buys precisely because they stop being the newest thing. The Watch 7 already delivers most of what mainstream buyers need, and it gets more appealing every time its price falls. If that's the tier you're considering, our [Samsung Galaxy Watch 7 review](/posts/samsung-galaxy-watch-7-review-flagship-smarts-under-200) breaks down what you actually give up by skipping the flagship.

If you already own a recent Galaxy Watch, this is likely a year to sit out. Generational smartwatch upgrades have been incremental for a while, and a new chip plus a bigger battery rarely justifies replacing a one or two-year-old watch that still holds a charge. The interesting question for existing owners is not the new hardware but how far last year's models fall, because that sets the trade-in and resale value of the watch on your wrist.

One caution worth keeping in mind: several outlets covering the event have flagged rising prices across Samsung's 2026 lineup. If the new watches launch higher than their predecessors, the value case for a discounted last-gen model gets stronger, not weaker.

## Buy or Wait?

Wait, at least until the morning of July 22. There is no scenario where buying a current Galaxy Watch in the days just before a confirmed launch works in your favor. Either the new model is worth the jump, or last year's watch is about to get cheaper. Both outcomes reward patience, and the wait is measured in days, not months.

The one exception is a genuine, verified clearance deal on a current watch that already beats what the older model will realistically drop to. Those show up occasionally as retailers clear stock ahead of a launch. If you find one, check it against the tracked price history before you commit, since a "sale" that only matches the usual street price isn't a reason to skip the newer hardware. For everyone else, mark the 22nd, watch the keynote, and let the launch tell you which watch is the buy.
