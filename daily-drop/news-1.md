Garmin just put a $199.99 screen-free band on shelves that does the one thing Whoop refuses to: it works without a monthly bill. The CIRQA went on sale July 24, and for anyone who has eyed a recovery-focused wearable but balked at renting it forever, that's the headline.

The CIRQA is Garmin's first band with no screen at all. It reads your body around the clock and sends everything to the Garmin Connect app on your phone, rather than lighting up on your wrist. That puts it squarely against Whoop, the band that popularized the screenless, subscription-first model. The difference is the price tag actually buys you the hardware.

## Garmin CIRQA: What the $199 Band Actually Does

Garmin announced the CIRQA on July 21 and made it available to buy from July 24 at $199.99 in the US, £179.99 in the UK, and €199.99 in Europe. It's on Garmin.com and Amazon now.

The band tracks wrist-based heart rate, Garmin's Body Battery energy score, Pulse Ox, stress, skin temperature, and sleep continuously. It auto-detects more than 80 activities, so a walk or a workout logs itself without you tapping anything. Battery life is rated up to 10 days per charge, which is roughly a week longer than most smartwatches and in the same league as Whoop's multi-day runtime.

The subscription question has a real answer, and it has a footnote. Core metrics, heart rate, sleep, Body Battery, stress, and the activity tracking, are free in Garmin Connect with no fee ever. That's the whole point of the product. Garmin does sell an optional Connect+ tier at $6.99 a month or $69.99 a year for extras like guided strength plans, nutrition tracking, and its Active Intelligence AI features. The band you buy is fully functional without paying Garmin another cent.

## What It Means for Buyers

Compare that to Whoop, where the band is effectively free but useless without an ongoing membership. Cancel the plan and the hardware stops. Buy a CIRQA, cancel nothing, and it keeps tracking your recovery for the life of the device. Over two years, that gap is hundreds of dollars.

The winners here are people who want continuous recovery and sleep data but hate the rental model, and existing Garmin owners whose watch data will pool into the same Connect app and Body Battery score. If you already trust Garmin's sleep and stress numbers from a watch, the CIRQA is the low-profile, all-day companion that fills the gaps when the watch is off the charger.

Who should ignore it? Anyone who wants a screen. This band shows you nothing on your wrist. No time, no notifications, no glanceable stats. If you want a display and structured training tools, a proper watch is the better spend, and our [Garmin Forerunner 165 review](/posts/garmin-forerunner-165-review) covers the runner-first option that still skips a mandatory subscription. The CIRQA is a data-collection tool, not a watch replacement.

It also lands on a theme worth watching. Between Garmin refusing to gate core metrics and the steady buyer backlash against fee-locked features, the pressure on "buy the hardware, then rent the software" is building. The CIRQA is Garmin planting a flag on the side of one-time payment.

## Buy or Wait?

If you specifically want a screenless recovery band and you've been holding off on Whoop because of the subscription, buy now. At $199.99 with free core tracking and a 10-day battery, the CIRQA is the most buyer-friendly version of this category to date, and there's no launch-window penalty since it's already at its standard price on Garmin.com and Amazon.

Wait only if you're not sure you want a screen-free device. This is a brand-new product line, so first-batch owner feedback on daily accuracy and app reliability is still thin. If a display matters to you at all, put the $199 toward a Garmin watch instead. For everyone else who's been priced out by monthly fees, the CIRQA is the clearest reason yet to stop renting your wearable.
