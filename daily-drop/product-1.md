Every summer it plays out the same way. A storm knocks the power out for six hours, your phone slides to 4 percent, and the milk in the fridge starts its slow march toward the trash. A 288Wh battery like the Anker SOLIX C300 won't run your whole house, but it will keep phones, a laptop, a CPAP machine, and a few lights alive long enough to ride out the outage. ## What Is the Anker SOLIX C300?

The Anker SOLIX C300 is a 288Wh portable power station: a battery in a box with a handle, built to charge your gear when there's no wall outlet nearby. It runs on LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) cells, the same chemistry Anker rates for 3,000 full charge cycles before capacity drops to 80 percent. Drain it flat and refill it every single day, and you're looking at roughly eight years before it noticeably weakens.

It pushes 300W continuous (600W surge) across eight ports: three AC outlets, a car socket, two 140W USB-C ports, a smaller 15W USB-C, and a USB-A. That 140W USB-C matters more than the AC outlets for most people, because it fast-charges a MacBook Pro or a Steam Deck directly, no power brick required. The whole thing weighs about eight pounds, so it's a two-finger carry from the trunk to the campsite, not a piece of furniture.

---

## Who Should Buy the Anker SOLIX C300?

This is a weekend-and-emergency battery, and knowing that upfront saves you from disappointment. Buy the C300 if you camp, road-trip, or tailgate and want to keep phones, a drone, a camera, and a laptop topped up for a couple of days off-grid. Buy it if you want an outage cushion that keeps the essentials going: a router, a few LED lights, phones, and a CPAP through the night.

Skip it if you're shopping for real home backup. 288Wh is under a third of a kilowatt-hour, which won't carry a refrigerator far, and the 300W ceiling rules out anything with a heating element. If whole-home is the goal, you want a 1,000Wh-plus unit and a much bigger budget. The C300 is the everyday, throw-it-in-the-car size, and it's honest about that.

## What the Anker SOLIX C300 Gets Right

- **Three real AC outlets, not just USB.** Plenty of 300W-class batteries skimp here. The C300 gives you full wall sockets for a laptop charger, a fan, or a string of lights, so you're not hunting for adapters.
- **140W USB-C both directions.** The main USB-C port fills a modern laptop at full speed and refills the C300 itself the same way, so one PD 3.1 cable does double duty.
- **Fast top-ups.** From a wall outlet, Anker and verified owners both put it at roughly 0 to 80 percent in under an hour. You can pull it off the shelf, splash-charge it the morning of a trip, and go.
- **LiFePO4 longevity.** That 3,000-cycle rating outlasts older lithium-ion power stations by years, and the cells hold a charge for months in storage.
- **Genuinely quiet.** Verified-purchase owners consistently report the fan rarely spins for small loads, and professional reviewers put it around 25 to 30dB under heavier AC draw. It won't wake a tent.

## One Thing to Consider Before Buying

The 300W output ceiling is the real limit, and it catches people off guard. Three AC outlets sound generous until you realize the combined draw can't cross 300W continuous. No microwave, no hair dryer, no space heater, no coffee maker, no full-size electric kettle. Those pull 700W to 1,500W and trip the inverter the instant you plug them in. The C300 is for electronics and low-wattage gear, full stop. There's also lineup confusion to sidestep: the C300, the DC-only C300 DC, and the newer C300X share a name but differ on outlets and price, so check the exact listing before you click buy.

## Anker SOLIX C300 FAQ

**Q: Can the Anker SOLIX C300 run a full-size refrigerator?**
Not for long, and probably not at all. A typical fridge surges well past the 300W inverter limit the moment its compressor kicks in. The C300 is built for phones, laptops, routers, lights, fans, and a CPAP, not major appliances.

**Q: How many times can it charge a phone?**
With 288Wh of capacity, most owners get roughly 15 to 25 full smartphone charges depending on the phone, or a couple of full laptop refills. That's enough to keep a small group's devices alive through a long weekend.

**Q: Is the C300 worth it over a cheaper USB power bank?**
If you need AC outlets or want to power something for a full day, yes. If you only charge USB devices, a large power bank costs less and fits in a bag. The C300 earns its price the moment you need wall outlets off-grid or a cushion during an outage.

The Anker SOLIX C300 is the right size of portable power for camping trips and short outages, with battery longevity and quiet, fast charging that punch above its price. Buy it when the price dips into the low $200s or below, and don't expect it to run anything with a heating element.
