Every summer, the same question shows up in the group chat: which $50 speaker do we bring to the pool? For the last two years the answer was "whatever JBL Go you already own." The JBL Go 5 wants to be the reason you finally replace it, and after digging through the spec sheet and the first wave of owner and press reviews, it mostly earns that.

## What Is the JBL Go 5?

The Go 5 is JBL's latest ultra-portable Bluetooth speaker, the size of a bar of soap at 101 x 77.4 x 43mm and 230 grams. It runs Bluetooth 6.0 with support for AURACAST, JBL's system for linking multiple compatible speakers into one synced stereo pair or a small speaker network. It's rated IP68, which is a step up from the splash-resistant ratings on older Go models: you can submerge it, not just spray it, and JBL backs that with a drop-proof claim too. Street price sits around $55.

## Who Should Buy the JBL Go 5?

This is a second (or third) speaker, not a home theater replacement. It's built for the person who wants sound in the kitchen while cooking, on the porch, or clipped to a backpack on a hike, someone who values "small enough to forget it's in your bag" over maximum volume. If you already own a Go 4 and mostly use it solo, the upgrade case is thin. If you're buying into this speaker size for the first time, or you want to pair two of them for actual stereo separation, the Go 5 is the one to get.

## What You Get for $55

- **IP68 waterproofing** means the sink, the pool, and the inevitable spilled drink are no longer a threat, a real upgrade from the splash-resistant ratings on older Go models.
- **AURACAST multi-speaker pairing** turns two Go 5 units into a genuine left/right stereo pair instead of just louder mono, something the Go 3 and Go 4 couldn't do.
- **Bluetooth 6.0 with LC3 codec support** keeps the connection more stable through walls and crowded rooms than the Bluetooth 5.x speakers it replaces.
- **A built-in strap loop and 230-gram body** make it genuinely pocketable for hikes, showers, or a beach bag without adding real weight.
- **Up to 8 hours of standard playtime**, with a Playtime Boost mode that stretches it toward 10 hours by trimming bass output, useful for an all-day outdoor hang where volume matters more than depth.

## What You'll Pay

At $55, the Go 5 sits right where you'd expect a flagship mini speaker to land, a few dollars above generic waterproof speakers and in line with what JBL has charged for the Go line for years. You're paying for the AURACAST pairing and the IP68 bump specifically, not a fundamentally different speaker. ## The JBL Go 5 vs. the Alternatives

The most relevant comparison is JBL's own **Go 4**, still widely available and usually a few dollars cheaper. If you don't care about pairing two speakers together and your old one hasn't already died from water exposure, the Go 4 remains a perfectly good buy and the harder one to justify skipping. The other common cross-shop is the **Anker Soundcore Mini 3**, which trades JBL's brand recognition and AURACAST support for a slightly larger footprint and, per owner feedback, a bit more bass out of the box. Neither of those has a review live on GadgetDrop yet, so treat this as a spec-sheet comparison rather than a head-to-head we've verified ourselves.

## One Thing to Consider

The lighting ring that gives the Go 5 its "ambient edge" look adds 40 grams over the Go 4's weight, and that's the one tradeoff worth knowing about before you buy. It's not a dealbreaker on a speaker this small, but if you're chasing the absolute lightest option for a thru-hike, the older, lighter Go 4 might still make more sense.

**Q: Is the JBL Go 5 loud enough for outdoor use?**
It's not going to fill a backyard party on its own, but professional testers and owner feedback consistently describe it as impressively loud for its size at moderate volumes, on par with or ahead of the Go 4. For a patio, a kitchen, or a tent, it's plenty.

**Q: Can I pair two JBL Go 5 speakers together?**
Yes. AURACAST support lets you link two Go 5 units for true stereo separation, something earlier Go models couldn't do without a JBL Partyboost-only mono link. You'll need two Go 5s specifically, not a Go 5 and an older model, to get the stereo pairing.

**Q: Is the JBL Go 5 worth it if I already own a Go 4?**
Probably not, unless the IP68 rating or AURACAST pairing solves a specific problem for you. Reviewers are consistent on this point: the upgrade is real but incremental, and it's aimed at new buyers more than existing Go 4 owners.

## Verdict

The JBL Go 5 is the easy pick if you're buying your first pocket speaker or want to actually stereo-pair two of them, less so if your Go 4 is still working fine. Buy it for the IP68 rating and AURACAST support; don't buy it just because it's newer.

If the price in the card above is sitting at or near that $55 mark, this is a straightforward yes for anyone shopping in this category for the first time.
