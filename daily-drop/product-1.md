You want a movie night in the backyard without dragging a TV outside, so a projector the size of a water bottle sounds perfect. The catch with cheap portable projectors is that most of them look great in the product photos and washed out the second there's any light in the room. The XGIMI MoGo 4 is the one people keep recommending anyway. This XGIMI MoGo 4 review sorts out who it actually fits, and the one spec that decides whether you'll love it or return it.

Quick note before we start: there are two versions. This covers the standard MoGo 4 (the roughly $399 to $499 LED model). There's also a pricier MoGo 4 Laser that runs closer to $599 and is a brighter, different product. If you clicked expecting the laser, this isn't that one.

## What Is the XGIMI MoGo 4?

The MoGo 4 is a battery-powered 1080p portable projector that runs full Google TV, the same interface you'd get on a good streaming TV. It's small enough to hold in one hand, weighs under two pounds, and sits on a built-in gimbal stand that folds out of the body and tilts a full 360 degrees. That stand is the trick that makes it feel effortless: point it at a wall or ceiling, and the projector squares up the image itself.

Inside, XGIMI rates it at 450 ISO lumens with a native 1080p panel and support for images up to 120 inches. It has a 12W Harman/Kardon speaker, licensed Netflix (a real pain point on cheaper Android projectors that force you to sideload), and an internal battery good for about 2.5 hours of video, which covers most movies with room to spare.

---

## Who Should Buy the XGIMI MoGo 4?

This is a projector for dark rooms and after-dark spaces, full stop. If your use case is a bedroom with the lights off, a basement, a tent, or a backyard once the sun is down, the MoGo 4 is close to ideal. The all-in-one design means there's no separate streaming stick, no fussing with a tripod, and no laptop tethered to it.

It's also a strong pick for renters and travelers. You can move it room to room, take it to a friend's place, or pack it for a trip without a bag full of cables. Students in a dorm get a big screen without mounting anything to a wall.

Who should skip it? Anyone hoping to watch during the day or in a bright living room with the lights on. At 450 lumens, ambient light will wash the picture out, and no setting fixes physics. If your only viewing time is Saturday afternoon with the blinds open, buy a TV instead.

## The Features That Actually Matter

- **Google TV, not a knockoff launcher.** You get the real Google TV home screen with proper app support and licensed Netflix, so setup is closer to a smart TV than a budget projector. That alone saves the sideloading headaches owners of cheaper models complain about.
- **The self-erecting gimbal stand.** Twist the body and a stand extends, letting you aim anywhere from the floor to the ceiling. Combined with fast auto focus and auto keystone, you go from "set it down" to "watching" in under a minute.
- **Real portability.** Under two pounds with an internal battery means no wall outlet required. Roughly 2.5 hours of playback covers a movie, and it doubles as a 6-hour Bluetooth speaker with the lamp off.
- **Harman/Kardon sound that carries.** The 12W speaker is genuinely usable for a small room or a quiet backyard, so you're not scrambling for a separate speaker on night one.

## XGIMI MoGo 4 vs. a TV or a Brighter Rival

If you mostly watch in a bright room, a fixed TV is the honest answer, and something like the [Fire TV Omni QLED](/posts/fire-tv-omni-qled-review-color-that-leaps-off-screen) gives you a punchier daytime picture for similar money, just without the pack-it-anywhere freedom. Among portables, the Anker Nebula Capsule Air is smaller and cheaper but dimmer and lower resolution, so it's the grab bag pick rather than the movie-night pick. Samsung's The Freestyle (2nd Gen) is the closest lifestyle rival, though owners note its picture is comparable while its smart platform is less flexible than Google TV. The MoGo 4 wins when you want one device that just works in the dark.

## One Thing to Consider

The brightness ceiling is the whole story here, and it's worth being blunt about it. 450 ISO lumens is fine to good in a dark room and disappointing anywhere with real light. Professional reviewers also measured a native contrast around 350:1, which is modest, so blacks look more dark-gray than inky. Owners echo both points: great once the lights are off, underwhelming otherwise. The auto keystone is convenient but occasionally needs a manual nudge when you aim it at an awkward angle. None of this is a dealbreaker for a dark-room projector. It's only a dealbreaker if you bought it expecting daytime use.

## XGIMI MoGo 4 FAQ

**Q: Is the XGIMI MoGo 4 bright enough for a living room?**
Only with the lights off or the blinds drawn. At 450 ISO lumens it's built for dark spaces, and any real ambient light washes out the image. For a bright daytime room, a TV is the better tool.

**Q: Does the MoGo 4 have Netflix built in?**
Yes. It runs full Google TV with licensed Netflix, so you sign in and stream without sideloading apps. That's a genuine advantage over cheaper Android projectors that block official Netflix.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**
XGIMI rates it at about 2.5 hours of video playback, enough for most movies. With the lamp off it works as a Bluetooth speaker for roughly 6 hours, and an optional PowerBase stand extends screen time further.
