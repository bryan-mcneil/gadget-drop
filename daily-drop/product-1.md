Picture a 500-inch movie screen floating in front of you on a cramped flight, invisible to the stranger in the middle seat. That's the pitch behind the XREAL 1S, and it sounds like science fiction. This XREAL 1S review skips the sci-fi framing and asks the grounded question instead: for $449, are these AR glasses actually better for you than a $150 portable monitor or the screen already on your handheld? Here's where they win, where they fall short, and who should walk away.

---

## What Is the XREAL 1S?

The XREAL 1S is a pair of AR display glasses, not a full headset and not a standalone computer. Inside each arm sits a Sony 0.68-inch Micro-OLED panel running at 1920x1200 per eye, up to 120Hz, tuned to a claimed 700 nits and roughly 108% sRGB color. Through the birdbath optics, that renders as a virtual screen up to 500 inches, floating a few feet ahead of you with a 52-degree field of view. XREAL's own X1 chip handles the image processing, and native 3DoF keeps the picture anchored in place when you turn your head, so the screen doesn't swim around.

The frame weighs about 84 grams, close to a chunky pair of sunglasses, and the open-ear speakers were tuned with Bose. The catch that defines everything else: the 1S has no battery and no operating system of its own. You plug it into a phone, laptop, or handheld over USB-C, and it mirrors or extends that device's display. Think of it as a wearable monitor, not a wearable PC.

---

## Who Should Buy the XREAL 1S?

The 1S makes the most sense for three kinds of buyer. First, frequent travelers who want a big private screen for movies on a plane or in a hotel bed without hauling a tablet stand. Second, handheld gamers: plug a Steam Deck, ROG Ally, or Switch into the glasses and you get a giant display that the person next to you can't see. Third, anyone in a shared or cramped space, like a dorm or a studio apartment, who wants a cinema-sized picture without buying a TV or clearing a wall.

It's a weaker fit if you mostly want a desk productivity monitor. The picture is sharp for video, but professional reviewers note that dense text at 100% scaling in Windows is legible yet tiring over long stretches. If your day is spreadsheets and code for eight hours, a real monitor is the calmer choice.

## Why the XREAL 1S Display Stands Out

- **1200p per eye, not 1080p.** The jump to a 1920x1200 Sony Micro-OLED panel gives noticeably crisper text and cleaner edges than the older 1080p XREAL One, which matters most for menus and subtitles.
- **A screen only you can see.** Because the image lives inside the lenses, you can watch a movie or play a game in public with genuine privacy. No shoulder-surfing, no glare fights on a laptop screen.
- **Plug-and-play with USB-C DP devices.** Any phone, laptop, or handheld that supports DisplayPort Alt Mode over USB-C lights the glasses up with a single cable, including recent iPhones and popular gaming handhelds.
- **Light enough for a movie-length session.** At 84 grams with three nose-pad sizes, verified-purchase owners report comfortable wear from about 45 minutes up to a couple of hours before they want a break.
- **Bose-tuned open audio.** The built-in speakers aim sound at your ears without sealing them, so you still hear a boarding call while the movie plays.

## What You'll Pay

At $449, the 1S sits in the middle of the AR-glasses market: pricier than entry display glasses, well below a full mixed-reality headset. What you're paying for over a cheaper pair is the higher-resolution Sony panels, the wider 52-degree field of view, and the in-house X1 processing that keeps latency low. Budget for one hidden cost if you wear glasses: XREAL sells magnetic prescription inserts separately, around $79, since the 1S has no built-in diopter dial. ## XREAL 1S vs. the Alternatives

The obvious step-up is the XREAL One Pro, which runs closer to $599 and adds a wider field of view. Unless you specifically want that extra peripheral coverage, most reviewers found the cheaper 1S delivers sharper 1200p optics, making the Pro hard to justify for the average buyer.

The more honest comparison is a plain screen. If what you really need is a reliable second display for work rather than a cinema you wear, a desktop or portable monitor will be easier on your eyes and your wallet. Our [BenQ GW2486TC review](/posts/benq-gw2486tc-a-monitor-that-saves-you-money) covers a no-fuss option that costs less than these glasses and never needs a host device tethered to it. Buy the 1S for private, portable, big-screen video. Buy a monitor for all-day desk work.

## One Thing to Consider

The biggest limitation is baked into the design: the 1S is a display, not a device. It has no battery of its own, so it draws power from whatever you plug it into, and a phone will drain faster while driving the glasses. You also need a host with DisplayPort Alt Mode. Most modern phones and handhelds have it, but many desktop graphics cards do not, so a gaming PC may need a powered HDMI-to-USB-C adapter.

Then there's the headline 3D feature. XREAL's REAL 3D can convert regular content on the fly, but reviewers report the frame rate drops to about 30fps with visible jitter when it's on. It's a fun party trick, not a reason to buy. Treat the 1S as an excellent portable 2D screen that happens to do 3D, and you won't be disappointed.

## XREAL 1S FAQ

**Q: Do the XREAL 1S glasses work with an iPhone?**
Yes. Recent iPhones that output video over USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode connect with the included cable and mirror the screen. Older Lightning iPhones need a separate adapter, so check your port before buying.

**Q: Can I use the XREAL 1S if I wear prescription glasses?**
Not directly, since there's no built-in focus dial. XREAL sells magnetic prescription inserts for about $79 in powers from roughly -8 to +4 diopters, which clip inside the frame. Factor that into the total cost if you need correction.

**Q: Is the XREAL 1S good for working, or just movies and games?**
It's excellent for video and gaming and workable for light productivity. For heavy text work like long documents or code, owners and testers find the display readable but tiring over hours, so a regular monitor remains the better all-day desk tool.

The XREAL 1S is the strongest portable AR display you can buy in 2026, and at $449 it quietly beats XREAL's own pricier One Pro on sharpness. Buy it for private big-screen video and handheld gaming on the move; wait or skip if you wanted an all-day work monitor or a standalone device.
